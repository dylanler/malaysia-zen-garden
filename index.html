About this garden

Taman Kenangan means garden of memories. It is a zen garden built from Malaysian childhoods of the late 80s to the mid 2000s: the peel of a nasi lemak bungkus, a waterfall picnic, rain on a zinc roof, gongs over padi, a loom on a longhouse ruai, a kolam at a doorway, a cellophane lantern held too high, and the fireflies at Kampung Kuantan.

Five cultures walk the same path with the same weight: Malay, Chinese, Indian, Kadazan-Dusun and Iban. Each place has one thing to touch, one instrument, and a few lines of memory. Every instrument in the garden shares one scale, so anything you play here is in harmony with anything else.

What you will not find: characters, brands, scores, timers, or anything that can be failed. People are present only as traces: a cup still steaming, slippers on a rock, a half-played board. Festival lights are cultural lights; nothing sacred is made into a toy. The sape heard faintly near the river belongs to the Orang Ulu, not the Iban, and is credited as such.

Everything here is procedural: the landscape, the objects, and every sound is synthesised in your browser. Real field recordings, hand-modelled objects and reviews by each community are the next steps.

Names and words in Kadazan-Dusun, Iban, Tamil and Chinese should be verified by native speakers before this garden is considered finished. If you see a mistake, tell us; that is how gardens grow.
